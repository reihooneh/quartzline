Inside the wall

Take a plate at 700 °C and cool it through 573 °C. The rim is thin and open to the kiln on both faces, while the middle sits on a shelf that is still warm, so the rim cools first. When the rim's quartz shrinks, the warmer middle holds it at its old size and the rim is stretched round its edge. Change the plate, the clay and the cooling rate, and watch the pull.

The worst of it

Slower cooling gives the middle time to keep up with the rim, so they pass 573 °C closer together. A wider plate, a heavier shelf or a stiffer clay all make it worse. The studio does this for a whole firing and any shape.

Why 573 °C matters: how the clay grows with heat

Most of the time clay grows steadily as it heats. Free quartz jumps in size at 573 °C, and cristobalite does at around 226 °C. Pass through either unevenly, with part of the pot above and part below, and the two parts no longer fit each other.
